Augustus Competitive Landscape

Twenty-three institutions are building some version of always-on dollar settlement; six are selling Augustus's actual product — a chartered balance sheet that lets a foreign bank clear USD without a correspondent chain. No scores moved this week. The week's launches were all on the network side: Visa Direct moved stablecoin prefunding from pilot to production, Mastercard and BVNK shipped their first joint product, and HSBC took its tokenised deposit service into the Middle East. Each extends a rail Augustus already prices in, none puts a bank balance sheet between a foreign FI and the dollar, and the overlap read is unchanged. Four structural moves that reprice the set

Not companies Augustus sells against — each changes what the buyer expects as standard.

Aug–Oct 2026 · live, scaling Swift's ledger became the neutral layer HSBC and Standard Chartered settled the first live cross-border interbank tokenized deposit on Swift's ledger; Citi followed with FAB and OCBC, then with DBS settled the first live weekend cross-border USD payment on it — Singapore to US, in minutes. On 4 Oct the ledger executed another live USD transfer in tokenized deposits, and Swift expects at least 19 banks on it by year-end across five major currencies. Augustus's core claim — a neutral bridge between institutions, versus single-bank walled gardens — is the thing Swift is now supplying to incumbents who never had to build it, and the roster is still growing.
Announced Jun 2026 · vendor picked 24 Sep · live H1 2027 Two bank consortia, now with a named vendor 17 banks are pooling tokenized deposits on a Clearing House network tied to RTP and CHIPS — TCH selected Quant this week to build it, the first US private-sector payment utility to commit to blockchain infrastructure at systemic scale — while a separate 21 systemic banks form a shared dollar stablecoin company for a 1H27 launch. Both make 24/7 programmable USD settlement an expectation — and both serve member banks' own customers, not the foreign FIs Augustus targets.
30 Jun 2026 Open USD inverted the float A 140-company consortium — Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Coinbase, BlackRock, BNY, Standard Chartered, Anchorage and Ripple among them — routes reserve income back to distributors instead of the issuer. Circle fell 12% on the news. It reprices the net-interest half of every stablecoin-adjacent business model, Augustus's included.
2026 charter cycle · challenged 2 Oct 2026 The OCC opened the gate — and is now being sued over it Erebor, Coinbase, Circle, Mercury, World Liberty, Revolut and OpenReserve all cleared OCC approval inside eight months, alongside Augustus's own conditional approval in May; 21 such charters have been approved or conditionally approved, at least 13 to crypto firms. On 2 Oct the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC and Comptroller Gould in Washington, asking the court to strike the March 2026 final rule and Interpretive Letter 1176 on the ground that the OCC cannot charter largely non-fiduciary firms as national trust banks. Read the charter type carefully: the suit targets the trust path, which is how Lorum, Anchorage and Circle are chartered or applying — not the full-service national bank charter Augustus, OpenReserve, Revolut, Erebor and Column hold. If it succeeds, the cheap route to a Fed-adjacent charter closes and the deposit-taking charter Augustus already has gets scarcer again.

How to read the scores

Commercial traction · 0–10
Paid, live volume: settled volume, revenue, deposits, named clients, corridor coverage. A funding round is not traction — a pre-operational charter scores 1–3 however well capitalised.
Technology buildout · 0–10
How much of the settlement stack runs in production: own core versus vendor, direct rail connectivity, tokenized settlement as a product versus a bolt-on.
Overlap · 0–10
Same buyer and same promise scores 8+; adjacent rails or a different customer scores below 5.
Internal file · deep / partial / none
Depth of Tiger's own diligence, from a sweep of Egnyte, email, attachments and OneNote. None means the read rests entirely on public sources.
